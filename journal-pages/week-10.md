# Week 10

## In-Class Activities

### Progress Report

This week, instead of presenting my work formally in class, I spoke with several classmates individually to get feedback on the current state of my project.

At this stage, my overall concept was becoming clearer, but I was still developing how to communicate the idea effectively and how the different technological elements would function within the design.

I shared my sketches, concept developments, and research with classmates and discussed some of the challenges I was facing. In particular, I wanted feedback on whether the purpose of the design was clear, whether the connection to deep-sea ecosystems was understandable, and how the concept could be communicated more effectively.

The feedback highlighted the importance of continuing to refine and develop the concept before committing to a final outcome. Several classmates suggested that I further explain how the technology and sensors would work, continue producing sketches and diagrams to communicate the idea, and ensure that audiences can easily understand the purpose and interaction of the design.

These conversations were valuable because they helped me identify areas that needed further clarification and confirmed that the project direction was becoming stronger. They also encouraged me to continue refining how the concept is presented so that the connection between the design, emerging technology, and deep-sea knowledge is communicated more clearly.


### Feedback Received

Some of the recurring feedback included:

- Continue testing materials before deciding on a final solution.
- Make sure the environmental data remains the focus of the project.
- Consider readability when selecting colours and materials.
- Continue refining the physical interaction.
- Ensure the final outcome clearly communicates the information.

The feedback helped confirm that my project direction was becoming stronger, but there were still practical decisions that needed to be resolved before the final showcase.

## Reflection on Other Projects

Although I did not spend much time participating in the Gallery Walk activities, I spent time looking through examples of previous projects, installations, and physical information design works for inspiration.

I found it useful seeing how other designers translated complex information into physical experiences. One thing I noticed was that many successful projects simplified the information they presented rather than trying to communicate everything at once.

This reinforced the idea that I may need to focus on only the most important environmental data rather than attempting to display every dataset I have collected.

## Action Plan

After reviewing feedback and reflecting on the current state of my project, I realised that this week was less about producing finished outcomes and more about planning the next steps.

The most important things I identified were:

- Finalising material choices.
- Deciding how the environmental information will be displayed.
- Improving readability.
- Refining the physical structure.
- Preparing for final production.

### Action Points

- Continue testing physical prototypes.
- Gather stronger references and supporting research.
- Finalise environmental datasets.
- Create a clearer information hierarchy.
- Begin preparing files for laser cutting.
- Develop a more resolved digital model.

## Independent Study

### Material Testing

This week I spent most of my time experimenting with possible materials and trying to understand how the final outcome might be constructed.

Initially, I wanted to create the fish using blue materials because I thought it would create a stronger visual connection to water.

However, I encountered several challenges with this approach. One issue was accessibility to equipment and support. The Design and Engineering Lab was extremely busy, so it took some time before I could complete my induction refreshers and receive assistance from technicians.

Once I was finally able to access the workshop and experiment with materials, I created a small prototype to test some ideas.

### Data Collection and Planning

While working on the prototype, I realised that before committing to a final build, I needed stronger supporting information and more reliable datasets.

As a result, I spent time collecting and organising environmental data related to my proposal. This included water quality information and environmental indicators that could potentially be incorporated into the final outcome.

This process helped me identify which information was most relevant and what I actually wanted viewers to learn from the project.

Rather than rushing into production, I decided that Week 10 would become more of a planning and preparation week.

### Digital Development

I also continued working on digital models to help visualise how the project could be assembled physically.

These models helped me think through:

- Construction methods.
- Material thickness.
- Information placement.
- Laser-cut assembly.
- Overall scale.

Although the models are still rough, they provide a useful foundation for future development.


## Reflection

This week was less focused on making and more focused on planning.

While I would have liked to make more visible progress, taking time to organise my datasets, test materials, and think about construction helped me better understand what still needs to be done before the final showcase.

One of the biggest lessons from this week was realising that the final outcome depends just as much on good planning as it does on making. By slowing down and focusing on material testing, data collection, and technical preparation, I feel more prepared to move into the final stages of development.

For Week 11, I want to focus on:

- Finalising materials.
- Refining the digital model.
- Testing laser-cut components.
- Improving readability.
- Organising datasets.
- Beginning final production.
