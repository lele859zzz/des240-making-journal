## In-Class Activities

### Project Statement: First Draft

#### Case Study Activity

This week in class we looked at the *Xeno Computer 0.1: Labor* case study and discussed how speculative design can use data to communicate larger issues and future possibilities. Through this discussion, I realised that my own project statement and supporting references were not fully aligning with my intended outcome.

One issue that became apparent was that many of my references focused on underwater installations, environmental experiences, and existing marine technologies. While these references were visually inspiring, they seemed to place more emphasis on the physical prototype rather than the environmental data itself.

This made me question whether my project was successfully communicating the importance of the data. My original intention was always to focus on environmental information and water conditions, but the references I selected may have unintentionally shifted attention towards the physical form of the outcome.

#### Draft Project Statement

During class, I worked on refining my project statement and clarifying the purpose behind my design.

My project explores environmental water quality data and how it can be communicated through an interactive physical experience. The aim is to make environmental information more accessible by transforming invisible water conditions into a tangible visual system. Through this project, I hope to encourage people to think about how environmental conditions influence aquatic ecosystems and recreational activities such as fishing.

#### Evaluation

After reviewing my statement, I realised that my project still lacked clarity in certain areas.

Things that felt strong:

- The environmental focus.
- The use of real-world data.
- The physical interaction component.

Things that still felt unclear:

- Whether the project is specifically about fishing.
- How the final interaction will function.
- How much information should be included.

#### Commitment Sentence

**I will create an interactive physical visualisation that communicates environmental water quality data in a way that encourages people to better understand aquatic environments.**

## Round Robin Rapid Reactions

I completed a version of this activity with friends outside of class.

I showed my project concept and current prototypes to a friend and asked for their initial reactions.

The first thing they noticed was the fish shape. They immediately understood that the project related to marine environments and aquatic ecosystems.

However, when I asked whether they thought the project was specifically about fishing, they were unsure.

This raised an important question:

**Does the project actually need to be specifically about fishing?**

Another friend suggested that perhaps the project could simply focus on environmental water conditions rather than limiting itself to fishing. They argued that the information could be useful for anyone interested in interacting with waterways.

This feedback made me less concerned about whether viewers immediately associate the project with fishing.

The feedback also reinforced that the overall concept is working, but there are still practical challenges that need solving.

For example:

- Determining text sizes.
- Improving readability.
- Choosing materials.
- Deciding how information should be arranged.

If I continue with laser-cut wood, I will need to carefully consider how text can remain readable while fitting within the overall design.

This highlighted a potential next step: testing scale and typography before finalising the physical outcome.

## Independent Study

### Digital Development

## Making Sprint

Before beginning the sprint, I identified that one of the biggest challenges in my project was clearly communicating how the final concept would function. While I had developed numerous sketches and ideas, I wanted to move beyond two-dimensional drawings and begin visualising the design as a physical object.

### 3D Modelling Experiment

To explore this, I created a simple 3D model of my design using Autodesk Fusion. The purpose of this experiment was not to create a final prototype, but to better understand the form, structure, and potential construction of the concept.

Through this modelling process, I explored:

* The overall shape and form of the design.
* How different components could fit together.
* Scale and proportions.
* Potential construction methods.
* Whether the concept could eventually be manufactured using laser-cut materials.

Although I only produced one rough model, the experiment helped me visualise the project much more clearly than sketches alone. It also allowed me to begin thinking about practical considerations such as material thickness, assembly methods, and how individual pieces could be fabricated.

This modelling exercise became an important stepping stone before learning and experimenting with laser cutting. By first creating a digital version of the concept, I was able to better understand how the design might translate into a physical outcome and identify areas that would require further development in future iterations.

### Reflection

This week helped me understand that my project direction is becoming stronger, but clarity is still the most important challenge.

The feedback showed that people understand the environmental focus of the project, even if they do not immediately connect it to fishing.

I also realised that my references may have unintentionally influenced my project towards the physical outcome rather than the environmental data itself. Moving forward, I want to make sure the data remains the central focus while the physical form acts as a supporting communication tool.

The 3D modelling experiments were particularly valuable because they allowed me to visualise practical considerations that were difficult to communicate through sketches.

For next week, I want to focus on:

- Refining the 3D model.
- Testing readability and scale.
- Exploring laser-cut construction methods.
- Clarifying the role of fishing within the project.
- Continuing to improve the project statement.
