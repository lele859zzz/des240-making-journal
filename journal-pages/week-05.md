## Documentation 

This week we had our Reflective Proposal and our Consultation with Leo. 

This is what I submitted for my proposal for this class: 

My project direction for the Data-Driven Visualisation Project comes from reflecting on my
Making Journal experiments, especially the Live Data visualisation, which I enjoyed the
most. This was where I actually felt like a designer, because it involved both technical
problem-solving and creative decision-making. While I also explored hands-on approaches, I
found myself more drawn to the digital side because it felt more accessible and easier to
experiment with. I liked being able to quickly test ideas and see changes happen in real time,
which made the process feel more active and engaging.

Working with live data also changed how I see design. It made me realise that design
doesn’t have to be fixed or final, but something that can continuously change and respond.
That idea stuck with me, and it’s something I want to explore further. I’m especially
interested in how data can be turned into something visual and expressive, rather than just
clear and informational. Ideas like data humanism and data physicalization stood out to me
because they focus more on experience and interpretation, which connects to how I like to
work.

My design style is influenced by deconstructivism, where I use fragmentation, layering, and a
bit of chaos. I usually start with sketching as a way to think through ideas quickly, rather than
planning everything in a structured way. Because of this, I’m not interested in making
something clean or perfectly organised. I prefer outcomes that feel more open, abstract, and
slightly unpredictable.

For this project, I want to explore marine environments through the lens of fishing as a
common human activity. Fishing depends on conditions that are not always visible, such as
water temperature, movement, and patterns of marine life. The idea is slightly speculative,
asking what if we relied more on live data signals to understand and predict fishing
conditions instead of direct observation. This shifts the focus from seeing to interpreting,
where data guides decisions and shapes understanding.

I plan to use real live data related to ocean conditions, such as temperature, wave
movement, or marine activity, and bring it into a p5.js sketch. From there, I’ll translate that
data into abstract visuals, like shifting shapes, layered forms, and movement. I’m more
interested in how the data influences visuals in unexpected ways than in accuracy.
The visual outcome will likely be a generative and abstract system that keeps changing over
time. It won’t directly represent fishing or marine life, but instead suggest it through
fragmented and shifting forms. This connects to my deconstructivist approach, where things
are incomplete, layered, and open to interpretation.

The main idea is to question how we understand environments that are not fully visible. For
the audience, I want it to feel slightly unclear but engaging, where they interpret what’s
happening rather than being given a clear answer. Instead of just presenting information, the
project aims to show that data can be expressive, emotional, and open to different
meanings.

## Reflection 

After discussing my proposal with Leo, I gained a clearer understanding of what the project is actually asking for. My initial idea focused more on creating an interactive artifact where users could collect or gain data themselves. However, through feedback, I realised the emphasis should be on using data as the foundation of the artifact and finding ways to communicate or visualise that data.

Moving forward, I want to investigate environmental factors related to fishing, particularly water quality and other conditions that contribute to safe fishing environments in Aotearoa. At this stage, I am interested in researching what factors determine whether a fishing area is considered safe and how this information could be translated into a data-driven visual outcome.

This research is still exploratory and not finalised, but it provides a direction for next week. I plan to continue investigating relevant datasets and environmental indicators while considering how they could be represented through visualisation rather than through user data collection.
