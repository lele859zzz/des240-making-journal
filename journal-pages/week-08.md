## In-Class Activities

### Progress Report

I wasn't able to make it to class this week, so instead I asked some friends from the class for feedback on my project and how they thought I should move forward.

The main feedback I received was that it seemed like I still hadn't fully decided what I wanted my final outcome to be. They suggested that I start finalising some ideas soon so I would have enough time to properly develop and refine my design. I have always been more interested in creating something graphic-design focused rather than a physical installation, but I was still unsure how I could make something like a poster feel interactive rather than simply creating a 3D object.

My classmates also gave me some technical advice and suggested that I create a simple prototype using inexpensive materials first, such as cardboard boxes, to test ideas before committing to a final direction. Another discussion point was the amount of information included in my poster. I personally felt that the poster was becoming too wordy and brought this up during my presentation slides. However, my friends disagreed and felt that the amount of information was appropriate, especially because the data needed to remain accurate and informative.

## Independent Study

For my independent study, I decided to experiment with creating a physical version of the project using cardboard as a quick prototype. I cut out simple fish shapes and labelled them to help visualise what I had been thinking about. My intention was to test whether a physical format would feel more engaging than a traditional poster. If the concept worked, I considered that the final version could potentially be made from materials such as plywood.

While creating these prototypes, I also started thinking about how the project could become more interactive. To test this, I made a very simple paper prototype to see whether the interaction I had in mind would work. Surprisingly, I found the idea much more engaging than I expected. It aligned closely with what I wanted the project to achieve and made me almost completely forget about the poster idea.

The interactive prototype felt simple, intuitive, and easy for people to understand. However, I then started thinking about whether the overall concept was still clearly connected to fishing, which was the original focus of the project. Since the project aims to communicate information about safe fishing locations and water conditions, I wanted to ensure that users could immediately understand its purpose.

Although I was concerned that some aspects of the idea might be confusing, I decided not to make any major changes yet. Instead, I plan to gather more feedback before making a final decision on the direction of the project.

## Reflection

This week helped me realise the importance of committing to a direction rather than constantly exploring new possibilities. While experimentation has been useful, I am reaching a stage where I need to make decisions so that I can continue developing the project in greater depth.

The interactive prototype was particularly valuable because it showed me a possible alternative to the poster concept while still communicating the information I wanted to present. It felt more engaging and accessible than some of my previous ideas, while still allowing users to interact with the data in a meaningful way.

Moving forward, I plan to gather additional feedback and begin refining whichever direction best supports the project's goals. My main focus will be deciding whether to continue developing the interactive physical concept or return to the poster idea and find new ways to make it more engaging for users.
