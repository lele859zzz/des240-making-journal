# Week 11

## In-Class Activities

### Journal Review

Unfortunately, I was unable to attend class this week. However, I spent time reviewing my own journal entries from Weeks 6–10 to make sure they were complete and accurately reflected the development of my project.

Looking back through the journal helped me identify several important moments that shaped my project:

#### Key Moment 1 – Shifting Away From a Traditional Poster

One of the biggest changes in my project was moving away from creating a standard poster. Through feedback and experimentation, I realised I was more interested in creating a physical and interactive outcome rather than a purely graphic design solution.

#### Key Moment 2 – Exploring Physical Interaction

As I developed prototypes, I became increasingly interested in how people could physically interact with the information. This shifted my focus towards creating an object that people could engage with rather than simply view.

#### Key Moment 3 – Focusing on Environmental Data

Throughout the project, I continued refining the environmental focus of my work. Although fishing was the original inspiration, I became more interested in communicating environmental water quality and conditions in a broader way.

---

### Practice Consultations

Although I was unable to participate in the in-class consultation activities, I spent time reflecting on how I would explain my project during the upcoming Studio Consultation.

I found that I can clearly explain the physical outcome and the environmental focus of the project, but I still need to improve how I explain the relationship between the data and the final artefact.

Moving forward, I want to focus on communicating:

- Why I chose environmental water quality data.
- How the physical form supports the communication of data.
- What I want audiences to learn from interacting with the project.

---

### Showcase Planning

As the final showcase approaches, I began thinking about how the project might be displayed and presented.

Because my project is becoming a physical object rather than a wall-based poster, I need to consider:

- Display height.
- Visibility.
- Audience interaction.
- Stability of the structure.

These considerations will become more important once the final prototype is completed.

## Independent Study

### Project Development

This week I focused on beginning the production process of my final artefact.

Unfortunately, my progress was slower than expected due to time management challenges. Other design projects, particularly Capstone, required a significant amount of attention, which limited the amount of time I could dedicate to this project.

Despite this, I spent as much time as possible working with laser cutting and material testing.

### Laser Cutting Experiments

My first laser-cut prototype required a long wait before I could access the equipment. Once I finally received the pieces, I was able to begin testing how the design would work physically.

![Alt text](../assets/week-11/Screenshot%202026-06-04%20225517.png)
*First Successful Piece for the Artifact*

The first prototype was cut using green acrylic and generally worked as expected.

However, when I created a second prototype using yellow acrylic, I encountered several problems. The design was much harder to see and some of the details became difficult to read. In addition, some sections of the design did not cut or attach as cleanly as expected.

![Alt text](../assets/week-11/Screenshot%202026-06-04%20225459.png)
*Raster Etch not showing up*

At the time, I was unsure whether the issues were caused by the material, the laser settings, or the design itself.

Because the technicians were extremely busy, it took some time to troubleshoot the problems. While waiting, I experimented with different materials and settings to better understand how the laser cutter interacted with each material.

### Material Testing

To better understand the cutting process, I also tested plywood samples.

![Alt text](../assets/week-11/Screenshot%202026-06-04%20225450.png)
*Testing on Wood*

This helped me evaluate:

- Cutting power requirements.
- Material thickness.
- Structural strength.
- Visibility of engraved information.

Testing wood also helped me visualise what the final artefact might look like if I chose to move away from acrylic entirely.

### Final Colour Testing

After several rounds of experimentation, I laser cut multiple colour variations including:

- Red
- Green
- Yellow
- Orange

Comparing the different colours side by side helped me evaluate which materials provided the best visibility and readability.

This stage was particularly important because the final outcome relies on people being able to clearly distinguish different sections of information.

### Assembly

Once the pieces were cut, I began assembling sections of the project and testing how the components fit together.

This gave me a much clearer understanding of:

- How the structure would be assembled.
- Where glue and supports would be required.
- Which sections might need redesigning.
- How the final object would look when completed.

![Alt text](../assets/week-11/Screenshot%202026-06-04%20225443.png)

### Project Statement

Alongside the physical development, I also spent time writing and refining my project statement.

The statement helped me clarify:

- The purpose of the project.
- The role of environmental data.
- The intended audience experience.
- The relationship between data and physical interaction.

## Reflection

Although this week was affected by time management challenges, I still made significant progress towards the final outcome.

The laser-cutting process taught me a lot about materials, readability, and physical construction. While several prototypes did not work as expected, these failures helped me identify practical issues that would have been difficult to predict through sketches or digital models alone.

The most valuable part of this week was moving beyond planning and beginning the actual production process. Seeing the project exist as a physical object made the concept feel much more real and helped me better understand what still needs refinement.

Moving into Week 12, my priorities are:

- Finalise material choices.
- Complete assembly.
- Refine readability.
- Finish the project statement.
- Prepare the final artefact for showcase.
